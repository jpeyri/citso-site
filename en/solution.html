The solution

From audit to crisis recovery

CITSO covers the full security of your Active Directory: understanding where it is exposed, fixing it, protecting it for the long term and, if necessary, rebuilding it.

The problem

Active Directory is the key to everything,
and ransomware's first target.

Whoever takes it takes everything. CISOs know their directory is fragile, yet it rarely gets fixed. For three reasons:

1Years of accumulated settingsForgotten rights, inherited delegations, over-powerful service accounts: the debt has built up, and no one has the full picture any more.
2Fear of breaking productionHardening the directory can break an application, a service, an authentication flow. Without visibility on the impact, people prefer not to touch it.
3Tools that list without fixingAudits produce lists of weaknesses. They don't say how to fix them safely, or how to roll back if something breaks.
What CITSO does

How CITSO fixes,
in four steps.

1Audit and monitor, read-onlyChecks are based on the ANSSI, CIS and MITRE ATT&CK frameworks. Nothing is written to the directory during the audit or during monitoring.
2Show the attack pathsCITSO maps the paths that lead to administrator accounts and gives a fix order: what to cut first.
3Fix with your approvalFixes run under lock, only when you approve them, and each one has its rollback ready. Actions that cannot be undone are blocked by default.
4Prove every verdictEvery finding comes with its evidence. Anything that could not be checked stays "not assessed": CITSO never shows "compliant" by default.
How attacks get through

Rarely through a spectacular flaw

Most often, it is a chain of details: a forgotten reset permission, an over-delegated service account, an administrator session left open on a server. Taken one at a time, nothing alarming. Put together, they lead to the domain administrators.

Why the directory

An attacker gets in through the network, takes over identities, then reaches the data. The directory is the unavoidable middle step: that is where CITSO stops them.

The Red Forest: what is it for?

In a typical Active Directory, the accounts that administer the domain controllers live in the middle of production: on the same workstations, in the same domains as the users. An attacker who gains a foothold in production sooner or later runs into them, and with them takes everything.

The Red Forest takes these accounts out of production. It is a separate administration forest, never joined to your forests: your forests trust it through a one-way trust; the reverse does not exist. Administrators enter it with strong authentication and administer production from it, and only from it.

Why CITSO uses these privileged accounts. Because they are the ones that control production. With them CITSO sees everything, including object permissions, and can revert a suspicious change, cut off a compromised forest and rebuild it. Placing them in the Red Forest, behind strong authentication and two signatures for anything irreversible, is what makes this power acceptable.

What CITSO actually brings

The technical building blocks of the on-premises platform.

Managed service accountsCITSO runs with managed service accounts (gMSA, dMSA): passwords managed by the domain controllers, rotated automatically, never known to a human. One account per purpose: audit, backup, SIEM forwarding.
End-to-end encryptionLDAPS and AES Kerberos connections to your domain controllers, encrypted credential vault, encrypted backups sealed by hash, encrypted transfers between the platform and consulting laptops.
VPN connectionForests at remote sites or subsidiaries connect to the platform over VPN when a direct trust is not possible. Traffic always starts from the platform; nothing comes in.
Dynamic controlSensitive Tier 0 changes (privileged groups, delegations, DC GPOs, PKI, AdminSDHolder) are reported within seconds and can be invalidated on the spot.
Granular or full rollbackGranular: every fix or change can be undone object by object, attribute by attribute. Full: point-in-time forest restore from the replica DCs and the vault, or automated rebuild.
Centralised consoleOne console for all your managed forests, up to ten: status and maturity, map and attack paths, action plan, just-in-time elevation, PKI, chained log and two-signature ceremonies.

Two ways to deploy

With a consultant

The consultant is only there to analyse, produce the reports and, if you decide so, apply fixes. Nothing permanent is installed on your side: they come with CITSO on their laptop and leave nothing behind.

In-house

You install the equivalent of a nuclear submarine in your IT: a platform on dedicated hardware, supplied by CITSO or by you, that is a black hole for the network. Nothing can reach it from production; your administrators access it only with strong authentication. It controls every high-level operation on your forests, and everything restarts from it in a crisis.

Sizing the platform

The platform always runs a CITSO orchestrator, a PKI and the Red Forest domain controllers. On top of that comes at least one replica DC per managed domain: its size therefore depends on the number of forests and domains (up to ten forests) and on the object volume of each.

Indicative guidelines for the orchestrator and the Red Forest, by object volume:

Orchestrator on Windows Server 2025, 50 to 100 GB of disk excluding backups. Each replica DC is sized like a production domain controller of the same size. The vault depends on directory size and retention period. Final sizing is agreed with you.

What you need

  • A Windows workstation or server with PowerShell 5.1. For a one-off audit, it does not need to be domain-joined.
  • An account with the extended read rights needed to see everything, including object permissions (ACLs) and event logs; CITSO uses it read-only and never writes.
  • Network access to the domain controllers: LDAP (389/636), Kerberos (88), and SMB (445) to read Group Policy.
