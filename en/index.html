What we put in place

CITSO resources, brought together on a single foundation

The orchestrator, recovery controllers, backups, forensic analysis and alerts all sit in the administrative forest. Your forests are connected to it by a one-way trust or a VPN, depending on your architecture.

Our approach

Active Directory holds your entire information system together. It deserves more than a once-a-year audit.

1AuditNearly 400 checks, a map of the directory and the paths an attacker would take to reach your administrators. Every finding comes with its evidence and the list of affected objects.
2SecureA remediation plan ranked by priority, applied in waves. Every change is approved before it is made and can be rolled back. From one campaign to the next, you see what remains to be done.
3Build the fortressThe accounts that administer the directory leave production for an isolated administrative forest, the Red Forest. The directory is monitored continuously. If a compromise occurs, CITSO isolates first and leaves the next decision to you.
The fortress

Six defences to withstand an attack and recover from a crisis

The goal is twofold: stop an attacker from reaching your most powerful accounts and, if they do get there, keep everything you need to rebuild.

Red ForestThe administrative forest hosts CITSO and your Tier 0 accounts. It is not joined to any production forest. Production trusts it, through a one-way trust.
BoundariesSeparate administrative zones, restricted groups, trusts with selective authentication and SID filtering. An account compromised in production cannot climb up to administration.
Tier 0 monitoringChanges affecting Tier 0 are reported within seconds. If a sign of compromise appears, CITSO isolates immediately. Isolation is lifted only after a manager signs off.
Safe HarbourA shelter prepared in advance: regularly tested isolation plans, recovery controllers kept apart, guided rebuild. During the crisis, nothing is erased.
Sealed backupsBackups of the administrative forest and the protected forests are encrypted, and their integrity is checked by hash. Someone in control of production cannot reach them.
Remote domain controllersDomain controllers on other sites, replicated under control and checked before they become too old to be usable. Enough to rebuild even after losing the main site.

The problem

Attacks rarely go through a spectacular vulnerability

Most often, it is a chain of details: a forgotten reset permission, an over-delegated service account, an administrator session left open on a server. Taken one at a time, nothing alarming. Put together, they lead to the domain administrators.
