Our principles

Standards, good practice and design rules

A tool with control over an organisation's Tier 0 becomes a target itself. CITSO aligns with public frameworks, applies to the directory the practices recommended by ANSSI and vendors, and holds itself to stricter rules than those it checks on your side.

Our rules

Six rules with no exceptions

1Green has to be earnedA check that could not read its data shows “not evaluated” or “not assessable”, with the reason. It does not appear as compliant. An ANSSI maturity level is only validated if all its points have been verified.
2The audit changes nothingCITSO reads the directory; it does not write to it. A remediation is a separate operation: it is described in advance, approved, logged, and its rollback is ready before it is launched.
3Isolate firstAt the first sign of compromise, CITSO isolates without waiting for approval. Only a manager, through a logged signature, can lift the isolation. The suspect forest is frozen, not destroyed, so changes can be reversed at lower cost.
4Tier 0 kept apartCITSO and your administrator accounts live in an isolated forest, the Red Forest, which is not joined to any production forest. Your forests trust it; the reverse does not exist.
5Two signatures for anything irreversibleRemoving a domain controller or rebuilding a forest requires two signatures and the CISO's approval. This is the only point at which CITSO waits for a human decision before acting.
6On your premises, offlineCITSO is installed in your infrastructure and runs without any external connection. No directory data leaves your premises, and reports open without a network.

Recommended practices

What a well-run directory should respect

These practices recur in the recommendations of ANSSI, Microsoft and CIS. The CITSO audit measures the gap between your directory and these benchmarks; the on-premises platform applies part of them itself.

Administration
  • Tiered model: an administrator account never logs on to a lower tier
  • Dedicated admin workstations
  • Separate named accounts, just-in-time elevation
  • Privileged groups nearly empty outside emergencies
Authentication
  • Kerberos with AES, RC4 and NTLMv1 disabled
  • LDAP signing and channel binding, SMB signing
  • "Protected Users" group for sensitive accounts
  • Managed local administrator passwords (LAPS)
Service accounts
  • Managed service accounts (gMSA, dMSA)
  • No SPN on a privileged account
  • No unconstrained delegation
  • Review of constrained and resource-based delegations
Key infrastructure
  • Offline root, hardened issuing authorities
  • Certificate templates reviewed against known abuses (ESC)
  • Controlled NTAuth publication
  • Expiry dates tracked, revocation available
Resilience
  • Offline, encrypted backups, tested regularly
  • Written and rehearsed forest recovery plan
  • krbtgt password reset twice after an incident
  • Backups newer than the tombstone lifetime
Monitoring
  • Advanced auditing on domain controllers
  • Alerts on privileged groups, delegations, GPOs and AdminSDHolder
  • Forwarding to a SIEM
  • Review of Tier 0 changes
A green indicator that checked nothing gives false comfort, and that is often where an attacker gets through. That is why our first rule exists.
Verdicts

Where a verdict comes from

For each of its nearly 400 checks, CITSO keeps the data read, the threshold applied, the method and the list of failing objects. The checks rely on public frameworks: the ANSSI Active Directory control points, the CIS recommendations and the MITRE ATT&CK matrix.

A verdict takes one of four states: compliant, non-compliant, not evaluated when the data could not be read, not assessable when it does not exist in the environment. The report always states how many checks were actually evaluated.

A finding is considered fixed when a later campaign has re-evaluated it and no longer finds it. A check that can no longer read its data is not counted as a fix.

Audit and monitoring run with the rights needed to see everything, including object permissions, but never write anything: no write primitive is available in this mode, and every access is logged.

Our guarantees

What allows us to say so

Sealed codeEvery delivered file has a cryptographic hash, checked at installation and at every backup. The slightest change is detected.
Independent verificationEvery change is reviewed by someone who did not write it and who reruns the tests themselves. Anything that cannot be rerun is not accepted.
A booby-trapped labA deliberately vulnerable Active Directory forest serves as a test bench. Every planted weakness must be detected, every remediation must be reversible, and the return to the initial state is verified.
