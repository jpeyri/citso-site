Since 2016

From CITADELLE SOLUTIONS to CITSO

CITSO stands for CITadelle SOlutions. The project began in 2016 with an idea that has not changed: build a citadel at the heart of the information system, around the directory, because that is the path the most serious attacks take.

The logo is a bastioned star fort, like those Vauban built across France. Each bastion covers the others; no blind spot.

2016

CITADELLE SOLUTIONS: an appliance that takes back control of the directory, cleans it and redeploys healthy domain controllers.

·

Years of work on compromised Active Directory environments, and as many lessons on what does, or does not, let you recover.

2026

CITSO: read-only audit, Red Forest, Tier 0 monitoring, Safe Harbour and guided rebuild, with assistants at every step.

What remains from 2016: the citadel at the heart of the system, taking back control of the directory, resetting to a safe state on demand, forensic analysis on a copy, the appliance that doubles as a shelter.

Public cases

Five documented crises, and what they say about Active Directory

Our clients' cases remain confidential. The cases below are public and sourced; they show the same mechanisms we encounter in the field.

NotPetya at Maersk (2017)

Every domain controller destroyed within hours

What happened. The destructive malware spread through SMB vulnerabilities and by reusing credentials stolen from memory. Replication between controllers, designed for resilience, protected nothing: they were wiped together. The directory could be rebuilt thanks to a single controller in Ghana, which happened to be offline because of a power outage.

What we take from it. Replication is not a backup. You need remote controllers and backups out of reach of the production network, verified before you need them.

Source: A. Greenberg, “The Untold Story of NotPetya, the Most Devastating Cyberattack in History”, Wired, August 2018.

The Conti affiliate playbook (2021)

An industrial method for becoming domain admin

What happened. A disgruntled affiliate published the group's internal procedures. They describe the same sequence, repeated from victim to victim: directory reconnaissance with AdFind, Kerberoasting of service accounts, Zerologon exploitation on unpatched controllers, then ransomware deployment once domain admin rights are obtained.

What we take from it. Every step relies on a weakness visible in the directory long before the attack: a privileged service account with an SPN and an old password, an unpatched controller, an overly broad delegation.

Source: Cisco Talos, “Translated: Talos' insights from the recently leaked Conti ransomware playbook”, 2021.

Ryuk in France (2020-2021)

From an admin account to encrypting the whole estate

What happened. CERT-FR documented the Ryuk campaigns, which hit many organisations, including healthcare facilities. Once elevated rights are obtained in the domain, encryption is deployed at scale; a variant observed in early 2021 spread across the network on its own through scheduled tasks.

What we take from it. The window between the takeover of Tier 0 and encryption is short. Detecting a Tier 0 change within seconds and being able to isolate immediately matter more than an end-of-month report.

Source: CERT-FR, “Le rançongiciel Ryuk”, CERTFR-2020-CTI-011, updated in 2021.

SolarWinds and “Golden SAML” (2020)

Tier 0 does not stop at domain controllers

What happened. After the compromise of the SolarWinds Orion software supply chain, the attackers obtained administrative rights on AD FS servers and stole the token-signing certificate. They could then forge valid authentication tokens, bypassing multi-factor authentication, all the way into online services.

What we take from it. Anything that issues or validates identities (AD FS, certificate authorities, cloud synchronisation) is part of Tier 0 and must be protected, audited and monitored like the domain controllers.

Source: CISA, alert AA21-008A, “Detecting Post-Compromise Threat Activity in Microsoft Cloud Environments”, January 2021.

Zerologon (2020)

A domain controller taken without any password

What happened. A flaw in the Netlogon protocol (CVE-2020-1472) allowed an attacker on the network to reset the machine account password of a domain controller, then take over the domain. The US agency CISA ordered federal agencies to patch within days.

What we take from it. Domain controllers must be the first servers patched, and their actual state must be verified, not assumed.

Source: CISA, Emergency Directive 20-04, September 2020; Microsoft, CVE-2020-1472 advisory.

What ANSSI recommends after a compromise

Rebuilding the core of trust

In January 2024, ANSSI (the French national cybersecurity agency) published the “Cyberattaques et remédiation” collection, including a technical volume on remediating Active Directory Tier 0. It structures the way out of a crisis in three stages: contain the attacker, evict them from the core of the information system, eradicate their footholds.

How CITSO fits in. Immediate isolation corresponds to containment. Safe Harbour, isolated restore and guided rebuild prepare eviction and eradication. Tier 0 monitoring covers the supervision phase that follows.

Source: ANSSI, “Cyberattaques et remédiation : remédiation du Tier 0 Active Directory”, 2024.
